## 1. Phase 1 — the cup-target lineage claim is confirmed, and is stronger than stated

The review's central correction is right. It is also understated.

`mass_in_cup(M)` is reproduced by the **pure analytic integral of each replicate's own fitted single
exponential**:

```
mass_in_cup(M) = c₀ · λ · (1 − exp(−M/λ))
```

with **no measured first-fraction term at all**. The directive described the construction as a
measured first fraction *plus* the integral of the remainder; tested directly, that form leaves a
constant absolute offset per cell, while the pure integral does not.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| rows reproduced | **432** |
| rows within 0.01 % relative | **427** |
| median relative error | **0.00003 %** |
| max relative error | 5.769 % (5 rows) |
| `conc_in_cup ≠ mass_in_cup / M` | **0 rows** |

All five deviating rows carry a published mass that is **duplicated across replicates** in Table S3
(four duplicate groups; e.g. 5-CQA BR 1/3 = 128.8190 appears under exp 14 rep 2, exp 14 rep 3 and
exp 15 rep 1). The cause is transcription duplication in the source table, not a different
construction.

**Consequence.** `CUP_CURVE_3` is `DERIVED_FROM_FITTED_KINETICS` and
`NOT_AN_INDEPENDENT_CUP_MEASUREMENT`. Comparing held-out error against it with held-out error
against raw fractions was not a like-for-like comparison, and the earlier claim that held-out
prediction "settles" the observation-operator question is withdrawn.

### Smoothing and aggregation

Fitting the source's own exponential and scoring it both ways gives the sharpest possible statement
of the problem:

| solute | raw-fraction residual SD | derived-cumulative residual SD | SD attenuation |
|---|---|---|---|
| caffeine | 10.71 % | **0.00 %** | ~4.5e5× |
| trigonelline | 16.74 % | **0.00 %** | ~3.5e5× |
| 5CQA | 12.86 % | **0.00 %** | ~3.6e5× |

The cumulative residual is not merely small — it is **exactly zero to machine precision**, because
the targets were generated by integrating that very curve. Scoring the source's exponential against
the derived cup targets is a tautology, not a measurement. The same curve leaves 10.7–16.7 % residual
SD against the raw fractions it was fitted to.

The three cumulative targets are also strongly correlated within a replicate (ρ = 0.61–0.98, mostly
above 0.86) because all three are integrals of **one** fitted curve. Three cumulative numbers carry
much less than three numbers' worth of information.

## 2. Phase 2 — the late-tail discrepancy is NOT robust

Primary set: 16 GL 1.7 shots, 5 experiments, 3 solutes. Bootstrap clustered by **experiment**, 4000
resamples, seed 0.

| solute | mean late residual | cluster-bootstrap 95 % CI | shots negative at f7/f10 | mean abs late | **robust?** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| caffeine | −2.58 % | [−7.95, +4.32] | 0.62 | 7.27 pp | **no** |
| trigonelline | −8.76 % | [−17.02, +1.85] | 0.88 | 12.13 pp | **no** |
| 5CQA | −3.75 % | [−8.30, +2.10] | 0.62 | 6.97 pp | **no** |

Criterion 3 (mean absolute late residual ≥ 2 pp) is met by all three. Criterion 1 (≥ 70 % of shots
negative) is met only by trigonelline. **Criterion 2 is met by none: every interval includes zero.**
The result is identical under all three level policies — full-profile, first-fraction anchoring, and
a common level per solute — so it is not an artefact of level convention.

**The clustering is what changes the answer.** Pooling fraction rows without clustering, as the
earlier viability screen did, produces mean late residuals of −4.6 to −9.3 % that look decisive.
With only five experiments and three replicates each, experiment-to-experiment variation is large
enough that the mean is not distinguishable from zero. The earlier number was not wrong; the
inference drawn from it was.

## 3. Model comparison — raw fractions as the principal score

Level anchored on fraction 1, scored on fractions 2,3,5,7,10. **Two different evaluation lineages
appear in this table and must not be read as one:**

- **BASE, BIEXP, SLOW_TAIL** are scored **leave-one-experiment-out** — parameters fitted on the
  four training experiments, scored on the held-out fifth.
- **SRC_EXP** is not. It is the **source's own single-exponential fit to the same shot it is
  scored on** — the lineage baseline, carried here to show what the published data reduction
  does. Its residual is a same-shot source-fitted residual, not a held-out one, and it is
  systematically favoured relative to the three held-out rows by exactly that difference.

The comparison that decides the paper question is BASE against BIEXP and SLOW_TAIL, all three
held out on the same folds. SRC_EXP is context, not a competitor on equal terms.

| solute | model | all-fraction MAPE | **late MAPE (f7,f10)** | late signed | derived-cumulative MAPE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**The current solver is already the best of the four on raw fractions, for every solute.** The
slow-tail extension makes held-out late-fraction error *worse* by 0.45–2.70 pp; the two-timescale
benchmark is worse still. The improvement rule fails on every limb for every solute (0/3 meet the
30 % relative bar, 0/3 the 2 pp absolute bar, 0/3 improve all-fraction error).

Fitted slow-tail parameters are boundary-pinned in 3 of 5 folds for caffeine and 5CQA and vary from
`α = 0.05` to `0.5` and `ratio = 0.02` to `0.5` across folds, so they carry no mechanistic reading.

Positivity and mass conservation hold everywhere tested: minimum predicted concentration
0.060–0.102 at unit inventory across `α ∈ [0, 0.5]`, `ratio ∈ [0.02, 0.5]`, cumulative extracted
mass monotone in every configuration, no failed fits, no positivity violations.

## 4. The finding that actually emerged — and it is not the one we went looking for

The strongly negative late-tail signature belongs to the **source's own single exponential**, not to
the mechanistic solver:

```
same-shot source-fitted lineage residual:  SRC_EXP  −13.0 %, −21.2 %, −16.5 %
held-out late signed residual:              BASE     +1.6 %,  −3.5 %,  +1.4 %
```

The two lines are not the same measurement. SRC_EXP's is a same-shot source-fitted residual and
BASE's is leave-one-experiment-out — which makes the contrast *stronger*, not weaker: the source's
exponential under-predicts the tail badly even when fitted to the very shot it is scored on, while
the mechanistic solver stays centred near zero on shots it never saw.

The single exponential under-predicts the tail badly and systematically. The two-grain mechanistic
model does not — its held-out late residual is centred near zero. And that same under-predicting
exponential is the curve that was integrated to manufacture the cumulative "cup" targets.

So the masking story survives in a narrower and different form than proposed: **the derived
cumulative targets are generated by a curve that is itself demonstrably wrong in the tail, and
integrating it hides that completely — the residual against the derived targets is exactly zero.** That is a statement about the
*source's data-reduction*, not about the puckworks solver, and it does not support a paper thesis
about the solver's temporal validity.

## 5. Why this closes Paper 1

The screen was authorised to decide whether the temporal lead supports a paper. It does not:

- the discrepancy it was built on is not confirmed once experiments are treated as clusters;
- the solver is already the best available model on the raw data;
- the one authorised mechanistic extension makes held-out prediction worse and is boundary-pinned;
- the flexible empirical benchmark also makes it worse, so the tail is not a missing-second-timescale
  problem in any form this campaign can see;
- the only robust signal is a defect in the source's fitted curve, on 16 shots from one machine, one
  bean and one grinder.

Retain the exploratory artefacts as a negative scientific record.
