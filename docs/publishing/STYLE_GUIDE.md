# Puckworks editorial style guide

Write in Tim Brewer's first-person singular voice for an intelligent lay reader: curious, precise,
lightly dry, and explicit about evidence boundaries. Define technical terms on first use. Typical
sentences are 12–22 words, paragraphs two to five sentences, and headings occur roughly every
300–500 words. Lead with the question, result, or constraint. At most one equation is allowed; define
each symbol, explain it immediately, and bind any numerical example to evidence.

Critique claims, mechanisms, and evidence—not people. State a community belief in its strongest
reasonable form, explain why it seems plausible, define the evidence it would need, and treat
alternatives fairly. Equipment names are allowed only when required by the evidence context. Never
write a recommendation, ranking, purchasing instruction, flavor prediction, or taste claim.

Use this uncertainty pattern where applicable:

> In this run, under [conditions], I found [result]. This supports [narrow claim]. It does not
> establish [larger claim]. The largest remaining uncertainty is [uncertainty]. The most useful next
> measurement would be [measurement].

Do not use: revolutionize, revolutionary, game-changing, journey, unlock the secret, shocking,
proves once and for all, science says, everyone knows, objectively best, perfect shot, bad barista,
delve, tapestry, groundbreaking, or “robust” without a defined statistical or engineering meaning.
Do not use “the model shows” without naming the exact artifact.
