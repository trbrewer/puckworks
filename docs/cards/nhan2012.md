# Model card: Nhan & Phu 2012 — batch caffeine yield vs time and temperature, and a successive-percolation power law

**Paper/thesis:** Pham Phuoc Nhan, Nguyen Tran Phu, "Effect of Time and Water Temperature on Caffeine Extraction from Coffee," *Pakistan Journal of Nutrition* **11**(2): 100–103 (2012). ISSN 1680-5194. No DOI printed. Asian Network for Scientific Information.
**Stage(s):** none (nominally *extraction* / *observables*, as a caffeine-inventory data candidate only) · **Kind:** n/a (calibration at best; nothing runtime)
**Status:** proposed (card-only)

## Scope and mechanism
An analytical-nutrition survey, not a process model. Ground, oven-dried *C. arabica* and *C. robusta* powder (5.0 g in 30 mL water, sealed beaker, water bath) is soaked for 5/10/15/30 min at 100 °C, and for 15 min at 60/70/80/90/100 °C; caffeine is isolated by thin-layer chromatography and quantified by single-wavelength UV absorbance against one caffeine standard. A third experiment runs five successive gravity percolations of *C. robusta* in an aluminium phin-style percolator (5 g, 30 mL boiling water per pass) and fits the per-pass caffeine release to a power law in pass number. A fourth measures moisture, total soluble sugars, and caffeine in three Vietnamese instant-coffee retail products against their label claims. There is no transport model, no flow field, no bed geometry, no pressure, and no grind characterisation of any kind.

## Governing equations
The paper contains exactly one fitted relation. Everything else is bench protocol. Transcribed as printed.

**(E1) Successive-percolation decay** (paper, Fig. 2 and Results §; the only equation in the article):

```
y = 1.7647 · x^(−1.5023),    R² = 0.9963
```

- `x` — extraction *sequence number* (integer pass index, 1…5), dimensionless. **Not a time coordinate and not a liquid-to-solid ratio**; each pass is one 30 mL charge of boiling water poured through the same 5 g bed and collected "until no droplet falling," with pass duration unreported.
- `y` — caffeine released in that pass, expressed as % of the dry powder mass (basis stated only as "on dry weight").
- Fitted on 5 points (n = 3 replicates each), *C. robusta*, boiling water, one percolator.

**(E2) Quantification, implied Beer–Lambert single-point calibration** (paper, Materials and Methods + Fig. 1; no calibration equation is printed):

```
C_sample = C_std · (A_sample / A_std),   C_std = 30 ppm, A_std = 0.495 at λ = 288 nm
```

- `A` — absorbance of the TLC-scrape eluate (band scraped into 4 mL distilled water, shaken, spun at 5000 rpm, 3 mL read).
- No calibration curve, no linear range, no LOD/LOQ, no recovery study, no path length stated. Back-computing gives an implied ε ≈ 3.2 × 10³ M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ (1 cm assumed), about **one third** of the accepted aqueous caffeine value (~9.7 × 10³ M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ near 273 nm) — consistent with reading off-peak. See Calibration and validation.

**(E3) Nothing else.** Sections on TLC development (ethyl acetate : hexane : acetic acid 80 : 20 : 1, 20 min), Duncan's multiple range test, and the Dubois phenol–sulfuric sugar assay are protocols, not equations. No term has been simplified away; there is nothing to simplify.

## Calibration and validation offered by the source
No model is validated. E1 is an in-sample fit to the five points it was drawn through; R² = 0.9963 on a monotone 5-point decay is not evidence of anything mechanistic, and no held-out pass, second bean, or second percolator was tested. The quantification chain is validated only in the sense that a standard was run on the same TLC plate.

Internal consistency, checked here:

- **E1 is arithmetically consistent with the narrative.** Summing E1 over x = 1…5 gives 3.104 % (paper: ">3.15 %"); pass 1 is 56.9 % of that total (paper: "no more than 59 %"); passes 2–5 are 43.1 % (paper: "only 40 %"); the 15-min 100 °C soak at 2.07 % is 66.7 % of the total (paper: "nearly two thirds"); predicted passes 4 and 5 are 0.220 and 0.157 % (paper: 0.23 and 0.16 %). The internal bookkeeping holds.
- **The abstract contradicts the body.** The abstract states that "about 90 % of caffeine has been removed from coffee powder by routine percolated." The body's own number for the first percolation is ≤59 % of total inventory; the 90 % figure is the *ratio of the first percolation to the 15-min 100 °C soak* (1.7647/2.07 = 85 %), not a fraction of the powder's caffeine. Anyone citing the abstract figure will be citing it wrong.
- **Tables 2 and 3 are headed "Caffeine content (%)" but measure yield, not inventory.** By the paper's own percolation total, the 15-min soak recovers ~2/3 of what the powder holds. The species comparison ("*C. robusta* has 1.7× the caffeine of *C. arabica*") is therefore a ratio of single-condition yields, not of bean inventories.
- **λ_max = 288 nm does not match caffeine.** Aqueous caffeine peaks at 272–274 nm with a broad (~40 nm) band; the registry's own `legasmuhammed2021` card records 271 nm (aqueous) / 274 nm (DCM). Fig. 1 shows a narrow spike labelled 288 nm, and the implied ε is ~1/3 of the literature value — both consistent with a mis-calibrated wavelength axis. Because standard and samples were read identically, this is largely **self-cancelling for relative quantification**, but it removes any claim to compound specificity: at 288 nm on a mis-set axis, whatever co-eluted with the purple UV-active band is being counted as caffeine.
- **Single-point calibration, no recovery.** One standard concentration, no linearity check, no blank silica scrape, no spike recovery through the scrape-and-elute step. Absorbances of the actual samples are never reported, so nothing can be re-derived.
- **The time series has no kinetic resolution.** The earliest point is 5 min, at which the paper's own numbers are already 96 % (arabica) and 94 % (robusta) of the 30-min value. Everything interesting happened before the first sample.
- **The temperature series is confounded between equilibrium and kinetics.** Yield rises 5.4 % (arabica) and 11.8 % (robusta) from 60 → 100 °C at a fixed 15 min. Whether that is a shift in the solid–liquid partition or simply slower approach to the same endpoint at 60 °C cannot be separated, because no time series was run at any temperature other than 100 °C. Duncan groupings put 90 and 100 °C together and 60 °C alone, which is all the resolution there is.
- **Statistics are minimal.** n = 3, Duncan's multiple range test, CV reported without SD or SE; error bars appear only in Fig. 2.

## Assumptions and validity range
- **Batch immersion and gravity percolation at ambient pressure.** No pressure gradient, no packed-bed hydraulics, no saturation front. Silent on every field of `MachineState`, `BedState`, and `GrindState`.
- **Beakers were cooled to room temperature before sampling.** Any species near its solubility limit at 25 °C would partially drop out before measurement. Caffeine at 3.45 g L⁻¹ (computed from the 15-min robusta soak) is far below its ~22 g L⁻¹ ambient solubility, so caffeine itself is safe — but the protocol is unusable for any less-soluble class, which is exactly what backlog item *multi-class solute chemistry* needs.
- **Grind is entirely uncharacterised.** "Ground to powder" with no mill, sieve, or PSD. The material cannot be placed on the `GrindState` axis at all, so none of the yields can be attributed to a particle size.
- **Roast state is not stated.** "Dried coffee grains … bought from coffee retailer," then dried at 60 °C to constant weight. Roasted is implied by context but never reported, and no roast level, colour, or mass loss is given.
- **Basis ambiguity.** Yields are printed as bare "%" ; the Discussion says "on dry weight," and separately reports 1.65 / 1.36 % powder moisture. Since the powder was dried to constant weight before extraction, the two bases differ by ~1.5 % relative — inside the reported spread but not reconcilable from the text.
- **Single species per condition (caffeine only)**, plus a lumped Dubois sugar number for the instant products. No acids, melanoidins, TDS, or sensory measure. No solubility, diffusivity, or partition coefficient is reported for anything.
- **The percolator is unspecified as a hydraulic object.** Aluminium phin, "collected until no droplet falling." No bed depth, basket area, hole pattern, or per-pass duration, so pass index cannot be converted to time or to a liquid-to-solid ratio at the bed.
- **Failure modes already realised in the paper:** the abstract's 90 % claim contradicting the body's 59 %; the 288 nm assignment; yield tabulated as content; the first time point already at ~95 % of plateau.
- **Where E1 breaks:** at x → 0 it diverges; it has no mechanism, no dependence on temperature, grind, dose, or pass volume, and it was fitted to one bean in one device. It is a curve through five dots, not a leaching law.

## Extractable data
Nothing is published as raw data or code; every number would be transcribed from the PDF. **Recommendation: transcribe nothing into `puckworks/data/`.**

- **Table 2** (time × species, 100 °C) — four points, all past the plateau. Carries no kinetic information. *Do not transcribe.*
- **Table 3** (temperature × species, 15 min) — the only marginally interesting grid, and the reason this card is longer than a one-line skip: a 5-point 60–100 °C series on two species is directly on the backlog's *observables: temperature effects* axis. But with no time series at any T other than 100 °C, equilibrium and kinetics are inseparable, and `pannusch2024` already supplies a fitted van 't Hoff K_i(T) for caffeine over 80–98 °C from a flow-through rig. *Do not transcribe; see the discriminating computation below.*
- **Fig. 2 / E1** (successive-percolation decay) — five points with error bars. Digitisable, but the abscissa is a pass index with no time, volume, or flow attached, so it cannot be mapped to any registry coordinate. *Do not transcribe.*
- **Table 4** (instant-product moisture / sugars / caffeine) and **Table 1** (label claims) — a consumer-labelling audit of instant coffee. No registry consumer. *Do not transcribe.*
- **Fig. 1** (standard caffeine spectrum) — worth keeping only as the visual evidence for the 288 nm flag, if a "known-defect catalogue" of analytical sources is ever assembled alongside `legasmuhammed2021` and `wale2023`.

**Named discriminating computation (not run here).** Evaluate `pannusch2024` Eq. 12, `K_i(T) = K_ref,i · exp[γ_i (1/T_ref − 1/T)]` with the fitted caffeine values `K_ref = 0.81`, `γ = −371 K`, over 60–100 °C: it gives `K(100 °C)/K(60 °C) = 0.888`, i.e. an 11 % *decrease* in the equilibrium liquid-side partition, whereas Nhan's robusta yield *rises* 11.8 % over the same interval. The magnitudes coincide and the signs do not. That is not a contradiction as it stands — Pannusch's model raises yield with temperature through `D_i(T)` and `h_sl`, not through `K` — but it does mean the sign of the pure-equilibrium temperature term is currently unconstrained by any registry source. Resolving it needs a batch soak run to true plateau (≥60 min) at each of 60/70/80/90/100 °C, which is precisely the experiment this paper did not do.

## Overlaps and conflicts
- **`pannusch2024`** — **superseded by it, comprehensively.** Multi-species (caffeine, trigonelline, CGA, TDS), 80–98 °C, flow-through, van 't Hoff temperature dependence, validated at 4.71 % MAPE on independent temperature experiments. Every question Nhan 2012 poses is answered there with a mechanism attached.
- **`schmieder2023`** — same apparatus lineage, time-resolved caffeine curves under controlled flow. Supersedes Table 2 outright.
- **`cameron2020.extraction_bdf`** (registered, runtime) — no interaction; Nhan supplies no rate constant, no grind, and no bed.
- **`taip2025`** — closest structural sibling in the registry: immersion, black-box, temperature-stepped, caffeine reported implausibly. Nhan is the better-behaved of the two (its arithmetic reconciles; Taip's 210.58 g L⁻¹ does not), but both sit outside the shot chain for the same reason — no flow, no pressure, no puck.
- **`legasmuhammed2021`, `wale2023`, `yu2021`, `mystkowska2024`** — the analytical-chemistry skip family. Nhan 2012 is the fifth consecutive member and fails the same discriminating test: **no time axis at kinetic resolution, and no flow field.** Note it is a *cleaner* paper than `legasmuhammed2021` or `wale2023` — no dimensional errors, no sign flips, internally consistent arithmetic — and still fails, which is the useful part: the triage rule is about the physics the source contains, not about its execution quality.
- **`cordoba2019`** — complementary as an immersion characterisation, but Cordoba at least reports TDS, EY, grind bands, and a factorial design. Nhan reports none of these.
- **`bruno2026`, `maille2024`** — same unmet need for a per-species solute-inventory contract field; Nhan adds a caffeine number to that pile but nothing structural.
- **Backlog *extraction: multi-class solute chemistry*** — does **not** advance it. One species, one protocol, no partition or diffusivity.
- **Backlog *observables: temperature effects*** — brushes it (Table 3) but cannot advance it, per the confound above.
- **No conflict with any registered component**, because nothing here is comparable to one.
