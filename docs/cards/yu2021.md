# Model card: Yu 2021 — DCM benchtop caffeine isolation, Costco Arabica vs Robusta

**Paper/thesis:** Yu Z., Reddy A. J., Wagh H., "Determining the Efficiency and Yield of Caffeine Extraction for Robusta and Arabica Coffee Beans," *Applied Cell Biology* **9**(3):75–80 (2021). DOI: 10.53043/2320-1991.acb90013.
**Stage(s):** none (nominally *extraction*, as a bean-inventory data candidate only) · **Kind:** n/a
**Status:** proposed (card-only)

## Scope and mechanism
An undergraduate organic-chemistry isolation write-up, not a process model. Two retail bean lots
(one Arabica, one Robusta) were boiled in water with calcium carbonate, the aqueous liquor was
liquid–liquid extracted into dichloromethane in a separatory funnel, and the solvent was removed
by recrystallisation and vacuum filtration to give solid caffeine, which was weighed and compared
against an arithmetic "theoretical yield." Product identity was assessed by melting point, IR,
UV-vis and TLC Rf. There is no bed, no pressure, no flow, no time resolution, no grind
specification, and no espresso; the only quantitative outputs are two recovered masses and two
percent yields. The paper self-describes as "this review" in both the abstract and the conclusion
while reporting bench work, and it is not clear the reported spectra were measured at all (see
below).

## Governing equations
No transport, kinetic or constitutive relation appears. Everything printed is stoichiometric
bookkeeping; transcribed as written, with the paper's own labels.

**(E1) Theoretical yield, Arabica** (Scheme 1):
`N_beans = m_charge / m_bean = 10 g / 0.12 g = 83.3`;
`m_caff,theo,ara = N_beans · m_caff,bean = 83.3 × 0.006 g = 0.4998 g`.
Symbols: `m_charge` mass of beans charged (g); `m_bean` mass of one bean (g); `N_beans` bean count
(dimensionless, not rounded to an integer); `m_caff,bean` caffeine per bean (g); `m_caff,theo,ara`
theoretical caffeine mass (g). The implied Arabica caffeine fraction is `0.006/0.12 = 5.0 % w/w`.

**(E2) Theoretical yield, Robusta** (Scheme 1, transcribed verbatim including the garbled
sentence "Theoretical Yield of caffeine from 0.4998 Robusta coffee beans"):
`m_caff,theo,rob = 2 · m_caff,theo,ara = 2 × 0.4998 g = 1 g`, i.e. `10.0 % w/w`.
The doubling factor is asserted from reference [15]; it is not derived from the 2.2 % w/w Robusta
figure stated two lines earlier in the same Scheme, and is inconsistent with it (see Parameters).

**(E3) Percent yield** (implicit, Table 2):
`Y = 100 · m_caff,exp / m_caff,theo`. Reproduces as printed: `100 × 0.4321/0.4998 = 86.5 %` → "86 %";
`100 × 0.8021/1 = 80.2 %` → "80 %".

**(E4) Percent error on melting point** (implicit, Table 1; reconstructed here because the paper
does not state the formula): the printed 0.88 % and 1.10 % are reproduced *only* by using the
**lower bound** of each range, not the midpoint —
`(227 − 225)/227 = 0.881 %` (Arabica) and `(227 − 224.5)/227 = 1.101 %` (Robusta, averaging the two
trial lower bounds 225 and 224). Midpoint-based errors would be 0.33 % and 0.55 %.

**(E5) Retention factor** (implicit, Table 3): `Rf = d_spot / d_front`. The formula, the mobile
phase, the stationary phase and the visualisation method are all unstated, and the column is headed
"*Expected Rf" — i.e. the values are not presented as measurements.

## Calibration and validation offered by the source
There is no model, hence no model validation. The characterisation that is offered does not
support the claims made from it, on the paper's own numbers:

- **The recovered masses are physically impossible as pure caffeine.** 0.4321 g from 10 g of
  Arabica is 4.3 % w/w and 0.8021 g from 10 g of Robusta is 8.0 % w/w. The paper's own Introduction
  cites 0.9–1.3 % (Arabica) and 1.51–3.33 % (Robusta) from ref [3]. The isolate therefore exceeds
  the total caffeine present in the charge by roughly 3–7×. Either the solid is mostly not caffeine,
  or the masses are not what they are described to be. The paper instead reads the 80–86 % figures
  as evidence that "the extraction method is relatively efficient."
- **The theoretical basis is internally inconsistent three ways.** E1 encodes 5.0 % w/w Arabica
  against the 0.9–1.3 % it cites; E2 encodes 10.0 % w/w Robusta against both the 2.2 % stated in the
  same Scheme and the 1.51–3.33 % cited in the Introduction; and 2.2 % is called "twice as much as
  Arabica," which would require Arabica ≈1.1 %, contradicting E1. Because the denominator is
  arbitrary, the headline percent yields carry no information.
- **The characterisation data appear to be computed, not measured.** Figures 1–2 are labelled
  "Literature"; Figures 3–6 are labelled "**Predicted**"; Table 1 is "**Predicted** Melting
  Temperature"; Table 3 is "***Expected** Rf." The Introduction states that identity was determined
  "via Avogadro's systems," a molecular-modelling package. Yet the Discussion attributes deviations
  in these same values to "equipment contamination," "procedural mistakes," and "inconsistencies of
  the melting point apparatus." A predicted spectrum cannot register laboratory contamination. This
  is the central incoherence of the paper and it is unresolvable from the text: it is not possible
  to determine which, if any, of the reported characterisation is experimental.
- **Percent error is computed from range lower bounds** (E4), which inflates the apparent
  agreement asymmetry; and the Discussion's claim that "the average melting temperature of caffeine
  from arabica coffee beans is 227–228 °C" quotes the *literature* column of Table 1, not the
  arabica trials (225–229).
- **The reference melting point is itself off.** 227–228 °C is well below the commonly cited
  anhydrous caffeine value (≈235–238 °C), so the 0.88 %/1.10 % errors are computed against a wrong
  target.
- **UV-vis reading is wrong on chemistry.** A 280 nm band is attributed to "the presence of a
  carbonyl group"; caffeine's ~272 nm absorbance is the purine π→π* transition, and an isolated
  carbonyl n→π* would be weak and further red-shifted. Figures 5 and 6 also show maxima near 200 nm
  and structure beyond 300 nm absent from the literature trace in Figure 2, and Figure 5 peaks at
  A ≈ 2.0, outside the reliable Beer–Lambert working range. None of this is discussed.
- **No replicates, no blanks, no standard, no HPLC or gravimetric cross-check, no recovery spike,
  no uncertainty of any kind.** n = 1 per bean type.
- Ancillary indicators: the Introduction states US adults consume "up to 5 mg of caffeine each day"
  (roughly two orders of magnitude low), asserts that "the extraction process took place under high
  pressures as this is extremely inefficient" (a self-negating sentence, and no pressurised step is
  described), promises a comparison to liberica beans that never appears, and closes with seven
  self-citations (refs [17]–[23]) to unrelated ophthalmology, dermatology, orthopaedics and algae
  work.

## Assumptions and validity range
- Whole-bean (possibly unground — never stated) aqueous boil at ambient pressure with a CaCO₃
  additive, followed by solvent partition into DCM. This is a decaffeination-style isolation, not a
  brewing process; the target is recovered solid mass, not a beverage.
- **Silent on** everything the registry needs: time, flow rate, pressure, bed depth, porosity,
  permeability, particle size distribution, fines fraction, temperature history, dose-to-yield
  ratio, water chemistry, and every solute other than caffeine.
- Cannot be located on the GrindState axis (no grind information at all), on BedState (no bed), or
  on MachineState (no pressure).
- Single lot per species, single trial, retail brands unnamed; no origin, roast degree, or moisture
  basis, so even the caffeine masses cannot be converted to a dry-basis inventory.
- Fails as a bean-inventory source specifically because the mass fractions it implies (5 % and 10 %
  w/w) lie outside any credible range for the species.

## Extractable data
**Nothing worth transcribing.**
- Table 1 (melting ranges), Table 2 (yields), Table 3 (Rf) are each 2–3 rows, labelled predicted or
  expected, unreplicated, and contradicted by the paper's own cited literature.
- Figures 1–2 are reproduced literature spectra (not this group's data, and copyright-encumbered).
  Figures 3–6 are "predicted" spectra of unstated provenance; digitising them would produce a
  computed spectrum of pure caffeine, which is available from any spectral database with proper
  provenance.
- No repository, no supplementary material, no code, no availability statement.
