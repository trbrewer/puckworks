# Model card: Toda 2022 — ultrasound- and pressure-intensified ethanolic extraction of oil from spent coffee grounds

**Paper/thesis:** Toda, T.A.; Santana, A.J.M.; Ferreira, J.A.; Pallone, E.M.d.J.A.; Aguiar, C.L.d.; Rodrigues, C.E.d.C., "Evaluation of Techniques for Intensifying the Process of the Alcoholic Extraction of Coffee Ground Oil Using Ultrasound and a Pressurized Solvent," *Foods* **11**(4):584 (2022). DOI: 10.3390/foods11040584. CC BY 4.0.
**Stage(s):** none (nominally *extraction* / *grind* on name collision and a PSD table only) · **Kind:** n/a
**Status:** proposed (card-only)

## Scope and mechanism
A solid–liquid **unit-operations** study on a **different material extracting a different solute with a different solvent**. The feedstock is spent coffee grounds (SCG) — the residue of *industrial soluble-coffee manufacture*, i.e. material whose water-solubles have already been removed at the factory — dried at 45 °C and charged with absolute (ET0) or 6 % hydrated (ET6) ethanol. The target solute is **lipid oil** (SCGO, for biodiesel/valorisation), not coffee solubles. Three contacting modes are compared at a fixed 30 min endpoint: stirred batch at 1:4 solid:solvent (U4), a jacketed fixed-bed cell with the extract recirculated by peristaltic pump at 140 mL·min⁻¹ and 1:15 (U15), and pressurized liquid extraction (PLE) at 10.35 MPa and 1:4. A 20 kHz probe delivers 0/200/400/600 W nominal. Outputs are endpoint oil yield, extract soluble-solids, residual oil in the raffinate, a solution retention index, plus morphology of the defatted solids (sieve + laser PSD, He pycnometry, bulk density, XRD, SEM) and a PCA over those six responses. The paper closes with a Treybal-style cross-current stage count.

The paper's own headline result is **negative**: neither sonication nor pressurization gives a significant yield gain over stirred conventional extraction; solid:solvent ratio and solvent hydration dominate both.

Against the registry's triage question for this genre — *does the source carry a time axis and a flow field?* — the answer is no on both counts. **Time:** every reported quantity is a single 30 min endpoint; the only kinetic content is Fig. S1 (two power levels, 1:5, ET0), which is supplementary and not reproduced in the article body. **Flow:** U15 recirculates liquid through a packed cell but reports no pressure drop, no bed depth, no bed porosity, no superficial velocity and no permeability; the pump rate is a mixing condition, not a flow measurement. This is the fifth consecutive skip in the "extraction ≠ extraction" family (legasmuhammed2021, yu2021, mystkowska2024, wale2023), and the first from the *process-engineering / valorisation* sub-genre rather than the analytical-chemistry one — the discriminating question generalises unchanged.

## Governing equations
Nine numbered relations appear; **none is a transport, kinetic or constitutive model.** Eqs. 1–5 are measurement definitions, Eqs. 6–9 are unit-op bookkeeping. Transcribed as printed, with the paper's numbering.

**(1) Sieve mean particle diameter** (ASAE S319.2):
`d_s = log⁻¹[ Σᵢ mᵢ · log(dᵢ · d_{i+1})^0.5 / Σᵢ mᵢ ]`
`mᵢ` mass retained on sieve *i* (g); `dᵢ` aperture of sieve *i* (mm). *As typeset in the article the summand is ambiguous (the log term is set as a fraction over Σmᵢ); the intended geometric-mean-aperture, mass-weighted log form is standard and is what reproduces d_s.*

**(2)–(3) Laser-diffraction mean of the bottom-tray fraction:**
`d_ld = 10^µ`, `µ = (1/100) Σᵢ qᵢ · (log₁₀xᵢ + log₁₀x_{i+1})/2`
`xᵢ` class diameter (µm); `qᵢ` differential distribution (%). Equipment-internal.

`d_avg` is then the **weighted average of d_s and d_ld** — the weighting is stated only as "the weighted average" and the weights are never given. Not reconstructible.

**(4) Calorimetric actual acoustic power:** `P_a = m·C_p·(dT/dt)`
`P_a` (W); `m` total mass of extraction medium (kg); `C_p` specific heat of medium (J·kg⁻¹·K⁻¹), from a mass-mixing rule over SCG/ethanol/water; `dT/dt` initial slope from manual thermometry every ≥10 s over 300 s at a fixed probe-relative position.

**(5) Power intensity:** `UI = 4·P_a / (π·D²)`, `D` = 25 mm probe face diameter. *Printed as "U I" in the equation, "PI" in the defining sentence, "UI" in the results — one symbol, three names.* Acoustic energy density `AED = P_a / V_extraction` (W·L⁻¹) is defined in prose.

**(6) Relative oil extraction yield:**
`Y_SCGO (%) = 100 · (M_SCG·w^SCG_SCGO − M_DS·w^DS_SCGO) / (M_SCG·w^SCG_SCGO)`
`M_SCG`, `M_DS` masses of charge and defatted solids (g); `w_SCGO` oil mass fraction of each. Note this is *relative* yield (fraction of the oil present that was recovered), not yield on charge mass.

**(7) Retention index:** `R* = (x_bRn + x_cRn)/x_aRn` — kg of adhered solution (solvent b + oil c) per kg of inert solids a in the stage-*n* raffinate.

**(8) Raffinate closure:** `x_aRn + x_bRn + x_cRn = 1`.

**(9) Inert fraction:** `x_aRn = 1/(1 + R*)`, from (7)+(8).

The cross-current cascade itself (Fig. 1) is solved by global and component mass balances with two modelling choices stated in prose and **not written as equations**: (i) the experimental `Y_SCGO` is reused as the **equilibrium ratio** of oil between phases, identically at every stage; (ii) stages 2…n are fed solvent numerically equal to the incoming raffinate flow (1:1). Both are strong idealisations of a batch endpoint measurement and neither is tested.

## Calibration and validation offered by the source
There is no model, so there is no model validation. What exists is a designed experiment with ANOVA/Duncan (p ≤ 0.05) and duplicate-or-better replication, plus a descriptive PCA. Three internal checks were run here `[RS]`; the first two pass, the third does not.

**Check 1 — Eq. 5 is self-consistent (passes).** With D = 2.5 cm, `4·23.07/(π·2.5²) = 4.70` and `4·73/(π·2.5²) = 14.87` W·cm⁻², matching the reported 4.70 and 14.90 endpoints. The AED endpoints imply a consistent 0.296–0.304 L extraction volume.

**Check 2 — the yield column reconstructs from the residual-oil column (passes, ±3.2 pp).** Under a pure-oil-removal balance on a dry basis (`w₀ = 0.234`, `M_DS = (1−w₀)/(1−RO)`), recomputed `Y_SCGO` matches all 16 tabulated values within 3.2 pp, and within 1.7 pp for 13 of them. The residuals are mostly positive (reported > reconstructed), the direction expected if non-lipid solubles are co-extracted — consistent with the authors' own interpretation of the PLE and U4-600 W soluble-solids results. The two U4-ET0 rows at 0 and 200 W run the other way (−1.7, −2.1 pp).

**Check 3 — the yield, soluble-solids and retention-index columns are not simultaneously consistent for U4 (fails).** Closing a mass balance over 1 g of as-fed SCG (dry 0.918 g, oil 0.2163 g wet basis) plus the stated solvent charge, with raffinate = inert·(1 + R*) per Eqs. 7–9, gives the total extracted solids implied by the measured SS. Because oil is a *subset* of extracted solids, that implied total must exceed the oil mass from Eq. 6. It does not:

U15-ET0 and PLE-ET0 close to 1–2 % relative — good agreement, and evidence the balance and the assumed bases are right. The U4 rows miss by 16–20 %, requiring 20 % *fewer* total dissolved solids than the oil the same table says was recovered. The discrepancy sits almost entirely in `R*`: reconciling SS with `Y_SCGO` under oil-only extraction demands `R* ≈ 1.13–1.40` for U4 against the reported 1.90–1.98. (The ET6 rows miss by more still, 20–36 %, but water partitioning between phases and the 100 °C oven-drying SS assay confound them, so they are not diagnostic.)

This matters because `R*` is not incidental: it is the **only** input that differentiates the extraction modes in the cascade design of §3.2, and the paper's engineering conclusion is stated as resting on it — PLE needs 4 stages vs 5 because its retention index is lower. If the U4 retention index is overstated by ~0.6–0.8 kg·kg⁻¹, the conventional/UAE-vs-PLE stage-count gap in Fig. 6 is not established. The paper does not report how `R*` was measured, beyond "determined experimentally."

**Check 4 — the skeletal densities fail a mixture-rule plausibility test.** Treating SCG as oil (ρ ≈ 0.92 g·cm⁻³, 23.4 % dry) plus an oil-free lignocellulosic matrix and requiring volume additivity, the measured `ρ_t = 1.52` implies a matrix density of **1.90 g·cm⁻³** — above cellulose (≈1.5–1.6), lignin (≈1.3–1.4), protein (≈1.3), and unreachable with 0.32 % ash. Running it forward instead, a matrix at 1.50–1.60 predicts `ρ_t(SCG) ≈ 1.31–1.36` and `ρ_t(defatted) ≈ 1.50–1.60`; the paper reports 1.65–2.41 for the raffinates, with 1.94, 2.10 and 2.41 exceeding any credible organic skeletal density. For reference, `maille2024` measured 1.27 g·cm⁻³ by He pycnometry on micro-ground roasted coffee, right where the mixture rule puts it. The authors themselves note the raffinate densities show "no standard behavior" relatable to the extraction conditions; the more likely reading is that the pycnometry is unreliable on solvent-wet or incompletely dried samples, and none of these numbers should be carried anywhere.

**Where the physical interpretation is weakest.** The sonication-breaks-particles claim rests on `d_ld` alone: for U4, sonication drops it from 63–67 µm to 8.5–9.4 µm, a 7× change, while `d_avg` moves 798 → 802 → 786 µm (ET0), i.e. not at all. Both can be true only if the affected material is a small mass fraction of the bed — which then makes it hard to attribute the 4.2 pp yield gain (U4 ET0, 0 → 600 W) to that breakage. The supporting evidence is SEM surface texture (qualitative, single fields) and higher bulk density (0.442 → 0.444 g·cm⁻³ for ET0 at 600 W — inside the quoted uncertainties, though Duncan-separated); XRD explicitly shows *no* crystallinity change. In U15 the same sonication produces no `d_ld` decrease at all (it rises to 118 µm at 200 W), and the authors correctly conclude the U15 yield gain is driven by solvent ratio, not cavitation.

**Minor defects,** recorded only because the paper's residual value would be bibliographic: Table 2's header sets the bottom-tray cut at "smaller than 297 µm" where §2.2 gives the analysed range as 0.25–290 µm; the Eq. 5 symbol is named three ways; §2.3.1 repeats the sentence defining the 30 min interval twice in one paragraph; reference [45] is cited as both "Chien et al." and "Gabriel et al." in adjacent sentences.

## Assumptions and validity range
- **Wrong material.** SCG here is *industrial soluble-coffee residue*, already aqueously exhausted of the solubles the registry cares about, then dried at 45 °C for 4 h 20 min from 62 % moisture. Its 23.4 % lipid content is a consequence of that (non-lipid mass removed at the factory), and it is above the range the paper itself cites for SCG. Nothing about its solute inventory, cell-wall state, or wettability transfers to fresh roast-and-ground coffee.
- **Wrong solute and solvent.** Lipid into ethanol at 45–50 °C. No aqueous phase except the 6 % in ET6; no acids, sugars, caffeine, melanoidins, or TDS.
- **Wrong length scale.** `d_avg` = 776 µm with PSD modes at 300/400/600/850/1200/1700 µm — coarser than filter grind and 2–4× coarser than espresso (cf. `melrose2019bed` d₄,₃ 200–360 µm). The sub-297 µm tray is a minority fraction throughout.
- **No time axis in the article.** One 30 min endpoint per condition. Fig. S1 (kinetics, 1:5, ET0, 188/611 W) is supplementary and not reproduced; the kinetic study the authors defer to is Toda 2021 (*J. Supercrit. Fluids* 177:105332), a separate paper.
- **No flow field.** U15's 140 mL·min⁻¹ recirculation is stirring by other means. No Δp, no bed geometry, no porosity, no permeability, no residence time, no Darcy or Forchheimer content anywhere in the paper.
- **Cascade idealisations untested:** endpoint yield reused as a stage-invariant equilibrium ratio; `R*` constant across stages; 1:1 solvent in stages ≥ 2; 99 % target. No stage-wise experiment was run to check any of these.
- **Silent on** every field the registry needs: `GrindState` (no fines fraction, no boulder radius; only a mass-basis sieve mean on the wrong material), `BedState` (no depth, area, porosity, k, κ or σ), `MachineState` (10.35 MPa is a static vessel pressure with no profile and no pump), `ShotResultState` (no EY, no TDS, no shot time, no beverage mass — `Y_SCGO` is a relative oil recovery, not an extraction yield in the coffee sense).
- Statistical validity is bounded by n ≥ 2 with Duncan separation at p ≤ 0.05; several "significant" density differences are third-decimal-place and should not be read as physically meaningful.

## Extractable data
**Nothing worth transcribing into `puckworks/data/`.**
- **Table 1** (Y_SCGO, SS, residual oil, R*; 8 conditions × 2 solvents) — the only quantitatively dense table, and the one that fails Check 3. Ingesting `R*` would import a retention index that cannot be reconciled with the yields printed beside it; ingesting the yields would import an ethanol/lipid recovery with no registry consumer.
- **Table 2** (d_avg, d_ld, ρ_t, ρ_a) — do not ingest. `ρ_t` fails Check 4; `d_avg` is unreconstructible because the d_s/d_ld weighting is never stated; `ρ_a` alone is uninterpretable without a sound `ρ_t`.
- **Figure 2** (PSD histograms, SCG and two raffinates) — digitisable and the closest thing here to registry-relevant data, but it is the PSD of coarse spent soluble-coffee residue. `hargarten2020` (dry/wet PSD by grinding degree and roast, with erosion controlled) and `maille2024` (17 materials, hybrid liquid+air laser PSD, D[4,3], D[3,2], sub-186 µm fractions, SSA) both dominate it on relevance and on characterisation depth.
- **Figures 3–7** (PCA biplots; XRD + relative crystallinity; SEM; stage counts; per-stage extract composition) — descriptive or design-output; nothing to transcribe.
- **Supplementary:** Table S1 (P_a, AED, UI with statistics) and Fig. S1 (the only kinetic curves in the work) are available at the MDPI article URL and were not part of this intake. Fig. S1 is the one item with a time axis; it is an ethanol/lipid batch curve at 1:5 and would still not be usable.
- **Availability:** "available on request from the corresponding author." No repository, no code, no raw data deposited. CC BY 4.0, so figures are freely reusable — moot here.

## Overlaps and conflicts
- **`maille2024` (calibration) — supersedes it on every shared axis.** Particle density by He (1.27 g·cm⁻³) and Hg (0.78–0.83), particle porosity 0.44–0.50, SSA 61–116 cm²·g⁻¹, hybrid PSDs for 17 materials, plus per-species extraction timescales. It also provides the external anchor that shows Toda's `ρ_t` values are high.
- **`hargarten2020` (calibration) — supersedes it** on wet/dry PSD and on any claim about mechanical modification of particles during contact with liquid; Hargarten separates swelling from erosion with statistical tests, on roasted coffee, at brewing temperatures.
- **`romancorrochano2017_extraction` / `romancorrochano2017_permeability` — supersede it** on particle-scale characterisation (ε_particle 0.50–0.65, sphericity ≈0.75, tortuosity, He + Hg) and are the registry's existing route from morphology to D_eff.
- **`melrose2019bed`, `wadsworth2026.permeability`, `brewer2026.pack_generator`** — no interaction. Packing density here is an incidental bulk-density measurement on coarse spent solids with no tamping, no confining stress, and a suspect skeletal density; it constrains no φ_p–k relation.
- **`cameron2020.extraction_bdf`, `schmieder2023`, `angeloni2023`, `pannusch2024`, `egidi2024`** — no interaction. Two-population saturated extraction and multi-class solute kinetics are untouched by a lipid/ethanol endpoint assay.
- **`foster2025.infiltration`** — no interaction, despite both involving liquid entering a coffee-derived porous solid. No front, no capillary pressure, no dry-bed condition, no first-drip; the material is pre-wetted by immersion.
- **Open backlog "extraction: multi-class solute chemistry"** — not addressed. One lumped lipid fraction, no species. `angeloni2023` remains the richest candidate.
- **Open backlog "grind: PSD models beyond bimodal; grinder transfer functions"** — brushed and missed. Fig. 2 is a sieve-plus-laser PSD, but of spent soluble-coffee residue at ~776 µm with no grinder, no setting, and no transfer function; it is a material characterisation, not a PSD model.
- **Open backlog "flow: Forchheimer/inertial correction"** and **"bed_dynamics: κ(t)"** — not addressed, and worth noting explicitly since U15 is nominally a packed column: no pressure drop was measured at any point in the study, so it contributes nothing to either.
- Competes with nothing registered; complements nothing; supersedes nothing.
- **Genre note for the triage heuristic.** This is the fifth consecutive skip in the "extraction ≠ extraction" family but the first from the *valorisation / unit-operations* sub-genre rather than analytical chemistry. The heuristic transfers without modification — time axis and flow field, not vocabulary — and the sub-genre is worth naming in `REGISTRY_STATE.md` alongside the analytical one, since SCG-valorisation papers are numerous, are indexed under "coffee extraction," and will keep surfacing.

## Implementation estimate
Nothing to implement, nothing to gate, no dependencies. Transcription would nominally be effort S (two tables, one PSD figure), but should not be done: Table 1's retention index cannot be reconciled with the yields printed beside it, Table 2's skeletal densities exceed physical bounds for the material, and the PSD is of the wrong material at the wrong length scale. Cite only if a "known-error catalogue" is ever assembled — Check 3 (a three-column mass-balance failure where the failing column carries the paper's engineering conclusion) and Check 4 (a mixture-rule bound on He pycnometry) are compact worked examples of audits worth running on any incoming table that reports yield, extract concentration and holdup together.

VERDICT: skip — an ethanol/lipid unit-operations study on industrial soluble-coffee residue with no time axis, no flow field, no bed geometry, no pressure drop, and a self-declared null result, whose retention-index column cannot be reconciled with its own yield and soluble-solids columns for the U4 conditions (implied total extracted solids 16–20 % below the oil recovered, implied R* 1.13–1.40 vs reported 1.90–1.98) even though the same column carries its cascade conclusion, and whose skeletal densities (up to 2.41 g·cm⁻³) exceed any credible organic value; maille2024, hargarten2020 and romancorrochano2017 dominate it on every characterisation axis it touches — effort S.
