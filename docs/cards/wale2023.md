# Model card: Wale 2023 — caffeine extraction & quantification techniques (narrative review)

**Paper/thesis:** Wale, K.; Girma, B., "An Overview of Techniques for Extracting Caffeine from Coffee for Quantification," *American Journal of Chemical and Biochemical Engineering* **7**(2):15–19 (2023). DOI: 10.11648/j.ajcbe.20230702.11.
**Stage(s):** none (nominally *extraction*, on the name collision only) · **Kind:** n/a
**Status:** proposed (card-only)

## Scope and mechanism
A five-page narrative review of **analytical sample-preparation chemistry**, not of brewing. "Extraction" here means transferring caffeine out of ground green beans into a solvent (dichloromethane, chloroform, methanol, ethanol, water) so it can be injected into an HPLC — Soxhlet, boiling-water fusion with liquid–liquid back-extraction, conventional heat reflux, and microwave-assisted extraction (MAE) are compared, followed by a survey of quantification methods (UV/Vis, FTIR-ATR, HPLC-UV, NIR, fluorescence, electroanalytical). There is no bed, no pressure, no flow, no time-resolved concentration, no dose, no beverage, and no espresso anywhere in the paper. The stage-name collision with the registry's `extraction` stage is the only reason this landed in intake and should be recorded as such: this is a *laboratory assay protocol*, orthogonal to the transport-and-kinetics sense the registry uses.

All quantitative content is secondary: Tables 1–2 are attributed entirely to reference **[17]**, and Table 3 to references [5], [10], [15], [20].

## Governing equations
**None are printed and none exist.** No transport, kinetic, equilibrium, or constitutive relation appears — no Fick, no Beer–Lambert (unusual even for this genre), no mass balance, no yield definition. The paper does not state how "% yield" is computed, against what mass basis, or on what dry/wet convention.

For faithfulness, the two implicit operations behind the tables are reconstructed below. **The review writes neither of them down**, and the first is contradicted by the review's own prose.

**(R1) Yield definition (inferred; Table 1 header vs §2.4 prose — the two disagree).**
The Table 1 header reads "% Yield of MAE Extract and % yield of Caffeine", giving two numeric columns per row: a lead value `y₁ ± SD` and a parenthesised value `y₂`. The header assigns `y₁` = extract yield and `y₂` = caffeine yield. The body text reverses this: *"When water was employed as a solvent, the Extraction yield was maximum (40.06%)"* — reading the **parenthesised** number as the extraction yield. Table 2 then relabels the `y₁` column outright as "% of Caffeine". Three mutually exclusive readings of the same two columns appear within two pages, and none is defined by a formula.

**(R2) Nothing else.** §§2.2–2.3 are bench protocols (volumes, times, bath temperatures), not equations. §3 and §4 are prose.

Symbols: `y₁` lead tabulated value, % of unstated basis; `y₂` parenthesised value, % of unstated basis; `SD` reported standard deviation, replicate count not given.

## Calibration and validation offered by the source
Nothing is validated. The authors measured nothing, ran no method comparison of their own, performed no recovery or spike study, and made no cross-check between quantification techniques on a common sample. The review's conclusions ("modern methods are better") are asserted from the compiled numbers.

Where the compiled numbers can be checked internally, they fail:

- **The same nominal condition carries four different values.** Water, 5 min, 50 °C, 800 W appears in Table 1 four times — set 1 as `7.25 (40.06)`, set 2a as `4.6±0.14 (26.29)`, set 2b as `7.25 (40.17)`, set 2c as `7.25 (39.81)`. The parenthesised value takes three values for one condition (40.06 / 40.17 / 39.81) and the lead value two (7.25 / 4.6, a 58 % discrepancy). The significance letters also differ between blocks for identical rows (`c` in set 1, `b` in 2b).
- **Table 2 fails mass balance against the paper's own §1.** A caffeine content of 7.25 % w/w (MAE) or 3.05 % w/w (reflux) is 4.5–10× and 1.9–4.4× the arabica range the same paper states (0.7–1.6 %), and the MAE figure exceeds even the robusta ceiling (4.0 %) by ~1.8×. It also contradicts the paper's *own* Table 3, where every quantification method returns 0.089–1.68 % w/w — a 2–80× internal inconsistency between Table 2 and Table 3.
- **The analyte is probably not caffeine.** Reference [17], the sole source of Tables 1 and 2, is titled *"Microwave-assisted extraction of **chlorogenic acids** from green coffee beans"* (DOI 10.1016/j.foodchem.2011.06.057). Green-bean CGA content of ~4–8 % w/w is consistent with the 3.44–7.25 lead values in a way caffeine is not, and a CGA-enriched extract at 34–40 % purity is consistent with the parenthesised values. The most probable reading is that `y₁` is **extract yield** and `y₂` is **% CGA in the extract**, and that the review has relabelled a chlorogenic-acid dataset as caffeine throughout. Either the attribution or the labelling is wrong; the review offers no way to tell which, and this is the entirety of its quantitative content.
- **Unit break in §2.3.** Hot-water infusion "1.6 mg/mL" versus Soxhlet "0.212 mg/L" is a 7547× ratio as printed, described only as "significantly higher". Presumably both are mg/mL (7.5×), but the paper does not say, and neither figure carries a sample mass or extract volume.
- **Table 3 is confounded, not comparative.** Each row is a different study on different beans; the 10–19× gap between the electroanalytical row (0.089–0.164 %) and the NIR row (1.44–1.68 %) is presented without comment, and cannot be attributed to method rather than sample.
- **Body contradicts conclusion.** §2.4 reports power raising yield from 3.7 to 7.25 (~2×) and temperature dropping it from 7.25 to 4.65 (~36 %); §5 concludes that "temperature and power supply have little effect on % Caffeine". §2.4 also contradicts itself inside one paragraph: *"As the Extraction power increased, the yield of Caffeine was also increased"* followed by *"it was not an increase in yield with an increase in microwave power."*
- Smaller defects, recorded because the paper's only residual value would be bibliographic: the permissible urinary caffeine level is printed as **12 g/L** (the IOC threshold was 12 µg/mL, i.e. 12 mg/L — off by 10³, and physically impossible); §1 states 75 % arabica / 24 % robusta [8,11,18] and then 60–40 % two sentences later [3], where [3] is a garbled entry ("Annual Review Anuario Rétrospective Retrospectiva (2012)") with no author or venue; reference [10] (Demissie *et al.*, UV/Vis caffeine in Hararghe green beans) is dated 2001 but is a 2016 paper; "caffeine … second only to oil in terms of global commodity trading" conflates caffeine with coffee and repeats a well-known myth.

## Assumptions and validity range
- **A review of secondary data with no measurement.** Validity is bounded by sources it does not reconcile and, for its main table, may have mis-attributed by analyte.
- Green, unroasted beans throughout. Silent on roast, and Table 3's own footnote-in-prose concedes the point (*"It is better to determination of caffeine from roasted coffee"*).
- Batch solvent contact — Soxhlet reflux, boiling immersion, microwave-heated vial. No flow, no pressure gradient, no packed bed, no percolation. **Silent on** every variable in `GrindState`, `BedState`, and `MachineState`.
- No time-resolved concentration. The MAE "time" sweep is three endpoint yields (2/5/10 min) at a single condition, on an undefined basis, from a source that disagrees with itself; it cannot be read as an extraction curve.
- Single species (caffeine), no acids, sugars, lipids, or bitter fraction; no solubility, diffusivity, or partition coefficient is given for any solvent.
- Solvents are organic (CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃, MeOH, EtOH) at ≤90 °C and ambient pressure. The one aqueous condition (100 °C, atmospheric, unconfined) is thermodynamically remote from a 92 °C / 9-bar percolation.
- Replicate count, sample masses, and species are unreported, so no uncertainty on any tabulated number is interpretable.
- Failure modes already realised inside the paper: four values for one condition; Table 2 exceeding both the paper's own bean inventory and its own Table 3; probable analyte mislabelling; a 10³ unit break in §2.3 and another in the urinary-threshold claim; body/conclusion contradiction on temperature and power.

## Overlaps and conflicts
- **mystkowska2024 (skip), legasmuhammed2021 (skip), yu2021 (skip) — same family, and this is the weakest of the four.** Mystkowska at least documented its search protocol and flagged its own unit ambiguity; legasmuhammed and yu at least ran an instrument. This paper measured nothing, states no method for source selection, prints no calibration curve, and its sole data table is likely for the wrong analyte.
- **schmieder2023 (registered) — superseded by it.** Schmieder gives caffeine `c₀ = 9.71 mg g⁻¹`, `λ = 23.09 g` in a cumulative-mass exponential on a DE1 Pro with known dose, basket, grinder and PSD. This paper has no brew and no time axis.
- **angeloni2023 (calibration) — superseded by it.** The 66-shot per-species espresso dataset flagged for the multi-class-solute backlog dominates on species count, time resolution, replication and machine control.
- **maille2024 (calibration) — superseded by it.** Maille supplies per-species extraction timescales (λ_fast/λ_slow) with CIs; nothing here has a time axis that survives its own internal contradictions.
- **bruno2026 (data-only) — superseded by it** for bean inventory, and bruno2026 is the reference set against which Table 2 fails.
- **cameron2020.extraction_bdf, pannusch2024, egidi2024 — no interaction.** Two-population saturated extraction and multi-class kinetics are untouched by batch solvent assays with no dose basis and no bed.
- **Open backlog "extraction: multi-class solute chemistry"** — not addressed. One species, no acids, no sugars, no kinetics. angeloni2023 remains the richest candidate.
- **Open backlog "observables: temperature effects; scale/measurement kernels"** — brushed and missed. The MAE temperature sweep (30/50/70/90 °C) is nominally temperature-dependent yield, but it is an unconfined batch assay on an undefined basis with a contradicted 5 min/50 °C anchor point; it constrains nothing about a percolating bed at 88–96 °C, and the paper's own conclusion disowns the temperature effect.
- Competes with nothing registered; complements nothing; supersedes nothing.
- **Terminology hazard worth recording once:** "extraction" in the analytical-chemistry literature means solvent isolation for assay, not solute release during brewing. Intake queries keyed on the registry stage name will keep surfacing this genre; the discriminating question is whether the source has a **time axis and a flow field**, not whether it has the word.
