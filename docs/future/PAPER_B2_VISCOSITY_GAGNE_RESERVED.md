## The mechanism (candidate, sign-degenerate with dissolution opening)

A decline in coffee-liquor viscosity as the dissolved-solids concentration falls over the shot lowers
resistance — a *rising* fixed-pressure flow contribution, weighted to the early high-concentration
interval. It carries the same net sign as dissolution-linked porosity opening, and both are driven by
the **same falling-concentration clock**, so the integrated *forward* trace cannot separate them.
Distinguishing them requires an independent time-resolved concentration measurement, or an
intervention that breaks the shared clock.

## Quantities (as drafted, to be re-derived with full provenance in the follow-up)

- Registered coffee-liquor viscosity–concentration closures: **Telis-Romero; Sobolík** (add full
  citations + validated concentration/temperature ranges in the follow-up).
- Early first-drip concentration near **15% solids**; viscosity ratio falls toward roughly **1.6** at
  the saturated bulk concentration; shot-integrated Darcy effect of order **~1%** (bounded,
  early-shot-weighted).
- Corroborating dataset — **Gagné**: eleven flow-controlled blooming shots on a commercial machine;
  post-bloom apparent resistance *declines* by a **median factor near 2.7**. This is the opposite-sign
  counterpart to the pressure-control Waszkiewicz trace, and it exhibits the same one-trace,
  many-explanations ambiguity (its per-shot yield reconstruction is endpoint-anchored / circular, so
  it constrains phenomenology, not mechanism).
