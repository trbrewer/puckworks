## Why the microscopy does not yield a retained growth bound

Figure2.5's caption and visual correspondence authenticate one particle, four
original frames and one common magnification. They also identify the black lines
in panels(e)–(h) as copies of the18s outline. Those lines cannot be measured as
the later coffee boundary. The native original frames show contiguous similarly
shaded coffee/bubble regions. Rounded protrusions at later right/lower boundaries
can be recognized as bubbles, but the underlying coffee edge is not visible enough
to provide a closed boundary with a source-supported conservative allowance.
Image-space rotation/focus and out-of-plane changes are not bounded either.
The first frame's bright central region also changes with time, making automatic
intensity thresholding an unsupported coffee-area definition. Segmentation stops.

This is a measurement-map limit, not evidence of zero growth. A difference in a
projected area, if qualified, would imply d_A(t)/d_A(18)=sqrt(A(t)/A(18)), not a
cubic conversion. A3Dvolume inference needs an additional supported geometric
assumption. The18s wet baseline cannot constrain earlier dry-to-wet changes; one
selected particle cannot bound the population maximum. Figure2 has no authenticated
shared scale or measurement protocol in the available poster, so its labelled
before/after silhouettes do not remove those limitations.
